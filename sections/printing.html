The Print List

Every structural element of the robot is printed: the chassis plates, the coxa brackets, the leg hip mounts, the femurs, the leg tips, and a small clip for the battery. Get these right and assembly is satisfying. Get them wrong and you'll spend the next section forcing things to fit.

Recommended Print Settings

The numbers below assume PLA on a well-tuned consumer FDM machine (Prusa, Bambu, Ender, Voron, Creality K-series). PETG is acceptable and slightly tougher; ABS is unnecessary and harder to print successfully without an enclosure.

  • MaterialPLA
  • Layer Height0.20 mm
  • Wall Lines4
  • Top / Bottom5 / 5
  • Infill25 % grid
  • Print Speed50 mm/s
  • Nozzle Temp210 °C
  • Bed Temp60 °C
  • Cooling100 % after L2
  • SupportsOnly where noted
Note · Why these settings

Four wall lines and 25 % infill is deliberate over-engineering for the loads involved. The reason isn't strength under static load; it's screw-pocket survival. Self-tapping M2 screws torqued into thin PLA walls will strip. The thicker walls give the screws something to bite into across many layers.

Orientation

How a part sits on the build plate determines almost everything about how it will perform. Layer adhesion is weakest perpendicular to layers; cosmetic surfaces face up; supports go where they can be removed cleanly. The orientation guide below is non-negotiable for the leg parts.

Chassis plates

Lay flat on the bed, large face down. No supports needed. The mounting holes will have a slight first-layer elephant's foot; this is fine, even helpful, as it creates a self-centring lead-in for the screws.

Coxa brackets

Stand on the long edge, mirrored left and right per the _l / _r file. This puts the layers across the screw axis, same reasoning as the hold brackets below. Print with tree supports under the overhang.

Hold brackets (hip mounts)

Stand on the long edge with the L-bend facing up. This puts the layers across the screw axis, so screw-pocket walls are reinforced with multiple layers. Print with tree supports under the overhanging arm.

Femur

Flat on the bed, servo-horn pocket facing up. The pocket needs no support if your bridging is well-tuned; otherwise enable supports inside the pocket only.

Leg (tibia)

Stand vertically with the foot at the bottom. Layer lines should run horizontally across the leg; this gives the best resistance to bending under landing impact. Like the coxa brackets, legs are mirrored left/right; keep the _l / _r files straight.

Warning · Tibia orientation matters

A tibia printed flat on the bed will print twice as fast and look identical to one printed upright, and it will snap the first time the robot lands a step. Do not be tempted. We have lost more legs to lazy orientation than to any other single cause.

Post-Processing

Plan to spend an hour cleaning up parts after printing. The goal is fit, not finish.

  1. Remove supports carefully

    Use flush cutters, not your fingers. Tree supports under the hold and coxa brackets can leave small stubs in the servo pocket; these will prevent the servo from seating fully if not cleaned out.

  2. Test-fit a servo into each hold and coxa bracket

    The servo body should slide in with light hand pressure. If it doesn't, file or sand the inside of the pocket until it does. Forcing a servo into a tight pocket and then screwing it down is how you crack a print at its weakest layer.

  3. Test-fit screws into all M2 holes

    Drive each screw in by hand to clear the hole and form a clean thread. Back the screw out before final assembly. You'll thank yourself when you don't strip a hole at the last moment.

  4. Check the chassis hole alignment

    Set the top and bottom plates against each other. Mounting holes should line up to within ~0.2 mm. If they don't, your printer has a calibration issue worth addressing before you continue.

  5. Mark left vs. right

    Femurs and hold brackets are not handed, but the coxa brackets and legs are printed as true mirrors: keep coxa_l paired with leg_l on the left side of the robot, and the _r parts on the right. Mark each print as you pull it off the bed; this becomes important during assembly.

Tip · Print one full set first

Don't queue up the entire build at once. Print one hold bracket, one coxa bracket (either hand), one femur, one leg first, and test-fit them with a single servo. If the dimensions are off, you'll have wasted one part instead of four. Once you confirm the fit, batch the remaining prints in groups of four.

What to Bring to Cebu

All printed parts. All of them. Even the spares. Even the failed test prints; bring them too, as a conversation piece. The on-ground programme assumes you arrive with the structural set complete and assembled into a working robot; we will not be printing replacements for you.
