What's in the Kit

A package will reach each visiting team by post before the programme begins. It contains the parts that are difficult to source consistently across three countries, most notably the PCB and the matched servo set. Everything else, you supply locally.

Note · Kit contents

Each delegate receives one (1) blank PCB, eight (8) matched SG90 servos (factory-fresh, same production batch), one (1) Arduino Nano (ATmega328P, USB-C), and a small bag of M2 hardware and JST connectors. The rest of the BOM below (including filament for printing, batteries, and consumables) is your team's responsibility.

3D-Printing Stock

You will print the structural parts yourself. STL files are distributed via the project repository (link in Credits). Recommended material:

  • MaterialPLA or PETG
  • Filament Required~120 g
  • Nozzle0.4 mm
  • Layer Height0.20 mm

Print settings are covered in detail in §03. ABS is permitted but unnecessary; the parts are not load-bearing under heat.

Warning · USB cable trap

A surprising number of USB-C cables shipped with phones in 2025–2026 are power-only and carry no data lines. If your Nano is recognised as a power sink but not as a serial device, the cable is the first thing to suspect, before drivers, before the board.

Optional but Recommended

  • Bench power supply: for first-power-on tests at 7.4 V before committing the battery. Saves you from the worst class of mistakes.
  • Helping hands / PCB holder: your soldering quality will improve immediately.
  • Logic analyser: overkill, but useful for debugging servo PWM if things go sideways.
  • Magnifying lamp: once you've used one, you can't go back.

Tip · Buy your spares early

One SG90 will fail. One M2 screw will strip. Order an extra of each before you begin, not after. Shipping a single replacement screw is more expensive than ordering ten with the original kit.
