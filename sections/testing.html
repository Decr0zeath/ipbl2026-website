Section 08 · The Last Mile

Testing & Calibration

Bench tests, gait verification, common failure modes, and the pre-flight checklist that confirms you're ready to fly.

The Standing Test

Before any walking is attempted, the robot must stand cleanly and hold its pose. If neutral isn't right, walking won't be either, and trying to debug a walk-cycle with mis-trimmed servos is one of the most demoralising experiences in this hobby.

Bench Test 01 · Power-On Stand

  1. Set the robot on a flat, hard surface

    Wooden bench or smooth tile, not carpet. The legs need to land predictably; carpet softens the landing and hides asymmetries.

  2. Apply battery power with SW1 in the OFF position, then switch on

    Powering on with the switch already closed creates a current inrush that can stress the buck. Connect, then flip.

  3. Observe the first two seconds

    Status LED illuminates within ~0.5 s. All eight servos drive to their trimmed neutral within ~1 s. Body should lift to its standing posture and hold steady. No buzzing, no shaking, no slow drift.

  4. Visual symmetry check

    Stand at the front of the robot and look down the long axis. All four femurs should be horizontal and at the same height. Sight along the body left-to-right; the chassis should be parallel to the bench, not tilted. If anything is visibly off, return to Section 07's calibration procedure.

Note · Servo buzz is a signal

A constant low buzz from a servo means it's being commanded just past its mechanical limit; the internal motor is fighting the end-stop. This will eventually burn out the servo. Adjust the firmware's range limits (the constrain(a, 10, 170) in setServo) or re-check the mechanical centring of that specific servo.

Bench Test 02 · Individual Servo Sweep

With the robot on the bench, open the serial monitor and step each servo through its range. This confirms the channel map is correct, no cables are swapped, and no servo is mechanically blocked.

  1. Send s 0 45 and observe

    The front-left hip should rotate the femur backward. If a different leg moves, your servo cables are mis-mapped. Power down, label-check, swap on the PCB headers.

  2. Send s 0 135 and observe

    Same leg, opposite direction.

  3. Send s 0 90 to return to neutral

    Femur returns to horizontal.

  4. Repeat for indices 1 through 7

    One servo at a time. Note any that hesitate, buzz, or have visibly less range than the others. A weak SG90 often shows up here; swap it in for a spare before the issue compounds.

Bench Test 03 · Walking

  1. Press the user button on the PCB

    Status LED turns off. Serial monitor reports State: WALKING. The gait begins immediately.

  2. Time a full body-length advance

    Mark the robot's starting position with masking tape. Let it walk one body-length forward (~12 cm). Record the time. The base gait should cover this in roughly 6–8 seconds. Significantly slower usually means a servo is slipping; significantly faster usually means the surface is too smooth and the feet are sliding without lifting the body.

  3. Check for straight-line travel

    After one body-length, the robot should still be heading in the original direction. A curve to one side indicates either a mis-trimmed leg, a weaker servo on that side, or a mechanically uneven leg (one tibia slightly longer than the others). Identify the offender; address it before continuing.

  4. Press the button again to stop

    The robot should return to neutral pose within one phase of the gait (about 1 s).

Common Failure Modes

The faults below cover roughly 90 % of what new builders run into. If yours isn't here, the troubleshooting guide on the project repository's wiki has more.

SymptomLikely CauseFix
Robot powers on, Nano LED lights, but servos don't move No power to the servo rail. Buck converter not delivering 5 V to J2–J9. Measure pin 2 of any servo header against pin 1. Should read 5 V. If it reads 0, the buck output isn't reaching the headers; check FB1 / jumper and the C1/C2 area.
One servo doesn't move; others fine Bad servo, bad cable, or unsoldered signal trace on that header. Swap the suspect servo's connector with a known-working header. If it now works, the original header has a bad solder joint. If it still doesn't, the servo itself is dead.
All servos behave erratically when more than two move at once Brown-out: servo current is pulling the rail down below the Nano's brown-out threshold. Re-verify that the Nano is on the +5V_LOGIC branch (after the ferrite bead) and that the servo rail is on +5V_SERVO (before the bead, with C1/C2). They share the buck output but should not share the same trace through the ferrite.
Robot walks but curves consistently to one side Asymmetric trim, or mechanically uneven legs. Measure each tibia tip-to-hip distance with calipers. Should be within 1 mm. If one is short, re-check that leg's screws and horn-seating. If lengths are equal, re-run trim calibration with the robot standing on a known flat surface.
Servo buzzes constantly at neutral Commanded angle is past mechanical end-stop. Firmware range limit too aggressive or trim too large. Reduce trim magnitude, or adjust the firmware's constrain() limits. Check that no print is touching the servo body.
Nano resets when servos start walking Power isolation between Nano and servo rails is incorrect. See "brown-out" above. This is the same root cause manifested differently.
USB upload fails with avrdude timeout Wrong bootloader selected, USB cable is power-only, or COM port not selected. Try the alternate "ATmega328P (Old Bootloader)" processor selection. Try a different USB cable. Re-select the port from Tools → Port.
Button press does nothing SW2 unsoldered, R2 pull-up missing, or D11 not configured as input. Measure D11 with a meter: should read ~5 V at rest, ~0 V while button pressed. If neither, the button or pull-up is wrong. If the readings are correct but the firmware doesn't react, re-flash.
Servos jitter even when not commanded to move Noise on the signal line, or insufficient decoupling on the servo rail. Confirm C1 and C2 are installed and correctly polarised. If still jittering, add a 100 nF cap across the suspect servo's signal-to-GND at the header.
Battery voltage drops rapidly under load Battery is undersized, internal cells are out of balance, or the pack is reaching end-of-life. Charge to full with a balance charger; measure each cell. If one cell is below 3.5 V at "full", the pack needs replacement.

Closing Up the Chassis

Once all three bench tests pass, install the upper chassis plate.

  1. Route all cables neatly

    Servo cables tucked along the inside of the chassis, away from the standoffs. Battery lead routed to exit through the rear cutout. Nothing should pinch when the top plate seats.

  2. Test-fit the upper plate

    Set it on the standoffs without screws. Confirm nothing protrudes, no cable is trapped, and the plate sits flat.

  3. Secure with eight M2 × 6 mm screws

    Four into the standoffs, four into the corner posts. Snug, not over-torqued; PLA threads strip easily.

  4. Re-test standing and walking through the closed chassis

    The closed robot should behave identically to the open one. If anything changed (buzzing, drift, a leg behaving differently), something is being pinched or shorted inside. Open it up and investigate.

Pre-Flight Checklist

Print this list. Tape it inside the lid of your travel case. Tick every item before you leave for the airport.

Bring with you, in working order
  • Assembled and tested quadruped (closed chassis, all eight servos installed)
  • 2× spare SG90 servos
  • One fully charged 2S Li-Ion battery pack plus a second pack if you have one
  • Balance charger compatible with your pack
  • Laptop with Arduino IDE installed and quadruped_base.ino already flashed once
  • USB-C data cable (confirmed working, not power-only)
  • Soldering iron, solder, flux, desolder braid (personal kit)
  • Multimeter
  • Small Phillips and slotted screwdrivers, M2 hex driver if applicable
  • Bag of spare M2 hardware (at least 10 screws of each size used)
  • Your build journal, paper notebook or digital; bring it
  • This manual, printed or on a device that works offline
Warning · Battery air travel

Most airlines treat 2S Li-Ion packs (≈ 7.4 V, < 100 Wh) as carry-on only. Do not pack the battery in checked luggage. Store each pack in a fire-resistant LiPo bag during transit. Familiarise yourself with your airline's specific rules at least two weeks before you fly; Cebu's customs has occasionally held shipments lacking documentation.
