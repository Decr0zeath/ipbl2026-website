Note · Kit contents

Each delegate receives one (1) printed PCB, one (1) Arduino Nano (ATmega328P, USB-C), and a small bag of the complete quadruped parts and components that needs to be soldered. The remaining items in the bill of materials below, including the twelve servos, batteries, capacitors, both buck converters, and consumables, are the responsibility of each team. Items shown in orange were goods the chosen courier does not allow to be shipped internationally; these must be sourced locally as well.

Important · The buck converters are not optional

The 2S pack delivers 7.4 V nominal. The SG90 servos are rated for 4.8–6 V, and the Raspberry Pi, which in turn powers the Nano, also needs a clean 5 V; neither can be supplied directly from the battery. This build needs two XL4015E1 modules (U2), not one: the first steps 7.4 V down to the servo rail at J1, the second steps it down again for a separate rail wired straight to the Raspberry Pi. Omitting either and wiring the battery directly to its load will overdrive the servos or the Pi past their rated voltage. Set and verify each converter's output independently before it is installed; see Section 06 for the full two-rail rationale and Section 07 for the calibration procedure.

Optional but Recommended

  • Bench power supply: current-limited, for first-power-on tests before committing the battery. See the buck converter verification step in Section 06, and repeat it for each of the two modules.
  • Helping hands / PCB holder: markedly improves soldering quality and consistency.
  • Logic analyser: not essential, but useful for debugging servo PWM signals if problems arise.
  • Magnifying lamp: substantially eases work with small components and fine solder joints.
