Half the Challenge, Still Ahead

A robot that stands and crawls off the bench is real progress, but it is only half of what iPBL asks of you. The other half is giving the quadruped a brain and a pair of eyes: a Raspberry Pi to think, a NoIR camera to see, and a proximity sensor to keep it off the walls. Figure 02 back in Section 01 already shows where that hardware sits in the system and how its rail is wired; mounting and powering it is a mechanical and electrical extension of everything covered here.

What runs on that hardware, the computer vision, the object detection, the logic that turns a camera frame into a decision, is deliberately outside the scope of this manual. It is not withheld by accident: iPBL is built around working that part out yourselves, with guidance rather than a script, and that part of the programme begins in Cebu.

Before you arrive

Treat the mechanical build, the PCB, and the firmware in Sections 01 through 07 as the foundation the rest is built on. Arrive with that foundation solid, a working knowledge of your own robot's wiring and code, and the Raspberry Pi, camera, and proximity sensor in hand. Setting up and programming all three happens at the event, with guidance, not a script.
