A blank PCB has been shipped to each delegate as part of the kit. The board layout is provided; the schematic that produced it is published below. Every component on the bare board should be traceable to a symbol on the schematic before the soldering iron is used.

Warning · Not everything arrives in the box

The courier used for this shipment classifies electrolytic capacitors, lithium battery cells, the buck converter module, and the twelve micro servos as restricted goods, so C1–C3, BT1/BT2, U2, and M1–M12 were withheld from every kit rather than risk the parcel being held at customs. These components must be sourced locally before soldering begins; see the bill of materials in Section 02 for exact part numbers and ratings.

Note · Two paths through this section

Some delegates may wish to redesign the PCB from the schematic; this is encouraged as a portfolio exercise, but a self-fabricated board should not be brought to Cebu. All teams will work from the standardised PCB during the programme. Soldering practice takes place on the supplied board.

The Schematic

The schematic is divided into four functional sections. Read each one and confirm you can locate its components on the bare PCB. The schematic PDF is in the project repository: quadruped-schematic-v1.pdf↓.

Section A · Power input and switch

The battery wires directly to the buck converter, not to the board: raw BT1/BT2 voltage reaches the buck converter's V_IN pin off-board, and only its regulated output enters the PCB, at J1, a 2-pin screw terminal (5 mm pitch). From J1, positive feeds through SW1, the master power switch, before reaching the rest of the board; the negative is tied to the board's common ground plane. SW1 is not a manufactured switch part: no latching switch small enough to fit the chassis could be sourced, so the position is instead a break in the trace bridged by two single-pin headers, closed by joining a male-to-male and a male-to-female jumper wire, and opened by separating them.

Warning · No reverse-polarity protection

This board revision has no protection diode on its power input. Connecting the buck converter's output backwards at J1 will feed reverse voltage straight onto the board's 5 V and ground nets with nothing to block it. Confirm polarity with a meter before every connection, and wire the screw terminal the same way every time.

Section B · Buck converter and rails

The buck converter module (XL4015E1) is wired in off-board, ahead of J1, rather than soldered to the PCB; the charger module (U3) is wired in parallel with it, both connecting directly to the battery pack's terminals, so the pack can be recharged without disturbing the buck converter or the board. What arrives at J1 is already regulated: a net labelled +5V on the PCB. A 100 µF capacitor (C1), soldered to the board itself at the J1 input, provides bulk filtering for that incoming rail before it splits into two physical branches:

  • A low-current +5V_LOGIC branch feeding the Nano's VIN pin directly (its 5V pin is left unconnected)
  • A high-current +5V_SERVO branch going to the twelve servo headers, decoupled by C2 and C3 (470 µF each)

Both branches share ground and run as separate copper traces from J1.

Section C · Microcontroller

The Arduino Nano sits in a socketed arrangement of two 15-pin female headers, one per row, so the module is not directly soldered, preserving the option to replace it. Its pin mapping is exactly as documented in Section 06. All twelve digital pins D2–D13 go to servo headers, so the status LED (D1) is instead wired straight to the Nano's 3V3 pin via R1 (330 Ω): it lights whenever the board has power and is not firmware-controlled.

Section D · Servo headers

Twelve identical 3-pin 0.1" headers in a row. Pin 1 of each connects to GND; pin 2 to +5V_SERVO; pin 3 to the corresponding Nano digital pin (D2 through D13). The signal traces run with a ground return alongside them where possible, but this is a single-sided, 1-layer board with no inner layer to route it on, so the routing is opportunistic.

Pre-Solder Checks

Before any solder touches the board, complete the following four checks:

Pre-Solder Checklist

  1. Inspect the bare board for defects

    Hold the board up to a light. Look for hairline scratches across traces, missing solder mask, mis-printed silkscreen, or oxidation on the pads. The boards were inspected before shipping but transit damage can happen. Photograph any defect and email the host team before you proceed.

  2. Continuity-test the power rails

    With a multimeter in continuity mode, probe between J1's positive pad and the near leg of the SW1 header. You should hear a buzz. Now probe between J1's positive pad and its negative pad. You should hear nothing. If you hear a buzz across J1 on a bare board, the board is faulty; do not proceed.

  3. Sort components into a tray

    Resistors with resistors, capacitors with capacitors, headers with headers. Read the resistor's colour code or use the meter to confirm it is the 330 Ω before soldering.

  4. Bend leads and pre-form parts

    For the axial through-hole resistor (R1), bend the leads at 90° to match the footprint spacing. This step requires some care but avoids difficulty with springy leads under the iron.

Soldering Order

Solder shortest-profile components first, tallest last. This keeps the board flat when you flip it to solder, with the components held against the table by gravity. The exact order:

Tip · Solder technique on this board

Heat the pad and the lead together for one second. Touch solder to the joint, not the iron. It should flow in, form a small concave fillet, and set within another second. A complete joint should take roughly three seconds end to end. If it is taking longer, the iron is too cold or the flux is exhausted; clean the tip and re-tin.

Polarity-Sensitive Components

The following components will be damaged, or will damage adjacent components, if installed backwards. Verify each one with a meter before applying power.

  1. D1 (LED). Long lead = anode (+). Flat side on plastic body = cathode (–).
  2. C1, C2, C3 (electrolytic capacitors). Long lead = +. White stripe on the can = – side.
  3. The Nano itself. The USB port faces the edge of the PCB labelled "USB"; do not insert it rotated 180°.

Post-Solder Inspection

Before any power is applied to a freshly assembled board, do this:

Post-Solder Checklist

  1. Visual inspection under good light

    Every joint should look like a shiny, concave fillet, not a dull grey blob. Dull joints are usually "cold"; reflow them. Blob joints often hide a short to a neighbouring pad; flux and re-touch.

  2. Continuity check: J1 positive to J1 negative

    With the SW1 jumper wires disconnected, probe across J1's two pads. There must be no continuity. If there is, you have a short somewhere on the input side, most often a solder bridge near J1.

  3. Continuity check: +5V_LOGIC to GND

    Probe the Nano's 5 V pin (in its socket) against GND. There should be no continuity. If there is, you have a short on the 5 V rail; check around the Nano socket pins and C1.

  4. Continuity check: +5V_SERVO to GND

    Probe pin 2 of any servo header against pin 1 of the same header. There should be no continuity. If there is (most commonly because an electrolytic was installed backwards and shorted), disconnect everything and find it before applying power.
