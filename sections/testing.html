The Standing Test

Before any walking is attempted, the robot must stand cleanly and hold its pose. If neutral is incorrect, walking will be as well; diagnosing a walk cycle with mis-trimmed servos is considerably more difficult than correcting the trim beforehand.

Bench Test 01 · Power-On Stand

  1. Set the robot on a flat, hard surface

    Wooden bench or smooth tile, not carpet. The legs need to land predictably; carpet softens the landing and hides asymmetries.

  2. Wire in the battery with SW1 disconnected, then join the switch wires

    Powering on with the connection already made creates a current inrush that can stress the buck converter. Wire the battery to J1 first, with the SW1 male-to-male and male-to-female wires unplugged from each other, then join them to power on.

  3. Observe the first two seconds

    Status LED is already lit; it is wired straight to 3V3, so it is on the moment power reaches the board. All twelve servos drive to their trimmed neutral within ~1 s. Body should lift to its standing posture and hold steady. No buzzing, no shaking, no slow drift.

  4. Visual symmetry check

    Stand at the front of the robot and look down the long axis. All four femurs should be horizontal and at the same height. Sight along the body left-to-right; the chassis should be parallel to the bench, not tilted. If anything is visibly off, return to Section 07's calibration procedure.

Note · Servo buzz is a signal

A constant low buzz from a servo means it is being commanded just past its mechanical limit; the internal motor is fighting the end-stop. This will eventually burn out the servo. Adjust the firmware's range limits (the constrain(a, 10, 170) in setServo) or re-check the mechanical centring of that specific servo.

Bench Test 02 · Individual Servo Sweep

With the robot on the bench, open the serial monitor and step each servo through its range. This confirms the channel map is correct, no cables are swapped, and no servo is mechanically blocked.

  1. Send s 0 45 and observe

    The front-left coxa should swing the whole leg sideways, toward the body. If a different leg moves, your servo cables are mis-mapped. Power down, label-check, swap on the PCB headers.

  2. Send s 0 135 and observe

    Same leg, opposite direction (away from the body).

  3. Send s 0 90 to return to neutral

    Coxa returns to centre.

  4. Repeat for indices 1 through 11

    One servo at a time. Note any that hesitate, buzz, or exhibit visibly less range than the others. A weak SG90 is often identified at this stage; replace it with a spare before the issue compounds.

Bench Test 03 · Walking

  1. Leave the robot undisturbed after power-on

    There is no start button; the firmware begins walking on its own a few seconds after boot, provided no serial command has been sent in the meantime. Serial monitor reports State: WALKING when the gait begins.

  2. Time a full body-length advance

    Mark the robot's starting position with masking tape. Let it walk one body-length forward (~12 cm). Record the time. The base gait should cover this in roughly 6–8 seconds. Significantly slower usually means a servo is slipping; significantly faster usually means the surface is too smooth and the feet are sliding without lifting the body.

  3. Check for straight-line travel

    After one body-length, the robot should still be heading in the original direction. A curve to one side indicates either a mis-trimmed leg, a weaker servo on that side, or a mechanically uneven leg (one tibia slightly longer than the others). Identify the offender; address it before continuing.

  4. Stop the robot

    Send any serial servo command (for example s 0 90) to return it to State: IDLE. Powering off stops it immediately as well.

Common Failure Modes

The faults below account for roughly 90 % of issues encountered by new builders. If a given issue is not listed here, consult the troubleshooting guide on the project repository's wiki.

SymptomLikely CauseFix
Robot powers on, Nano LED lights, but servos do not move No power to the servo rail. Buck converter not delivering 5 V to J2–J13. Measure pin 2 of any servo header against pin 1. Should read 5 V. If it reads 0, the buck output is not reaching the headers; check the C2/C3 area.
One servo does not move; others fine Bad servo, bad cable, or unsoldered signal trace on that header. Swap the suspect servo's connector with a known-working header. If it now works, the original header has a bad solder joint. If it still does not, the servo itself is dead.
All servos behave erratically when more than two move at once Brown-out: servo current is pulling the rail down below the Nano's brown-out threshold. Re-verify that the Nano is on the +5V_LOGIC branch and that the servo rail is on +5V_SERVO (with C2/C3). They share the buck output but run as separate copper traces, and should not be bridged.
Robot walks but curves consistently to one side Asymmetric trim, or mechanically uneven legs. Measure each tibia tip-to-hip distance with calipers. Should be within 1 mm. If one is short, re-check that leg's screws and horn-seating. If lengths are equal, re-run trim calibration with the robot standing on a known flat surface.
Servo buzzes constantly at neutral Commanded angle is past mechanical end-stop. Firmware range limit too aggressive or trim too large. Reduce trim magnitude, or adjust the firmware's constrain() limits. Check that no print is touching the servo body.
Nano resets when servos start walking Power isolation between Nano and servo rails is incorrect. See "brown-out" above. This is the same root cause manifested differently.
USB upload fails with avrdude timeout Wrong bootloader selected, USB cable is power-only, or COM port not selected. Try the alternate "ATmega328P (Old Bootloader)" processor selection. Try a different USB cable. Re-select the port from Tools → Port.
Robot never starts walking Auto-walk delay logic not reached because a serial command keeps resetting it, or firmware did not flash correctly. Close the serial monitor and stop sending commands; the gait should begin within a few seconds of the last one. If it still does not, re-flash and confirm the "Quadruped ready" boot message appears.
Servos jitter even when not commanded to move Noise on the signal line, or insufficient decoupling on the servo rail. Confirm C2 and C3 are installed and correctly polarised. If still jittering, add a small ceramic cap across the suspect servo's signal-to-GND at the header.
Battery voltage drops rapidly under load Battery is undersized, internal cells are out of balance, or the pack is reaching end-of-life. Charge to full with a balance charger; measure each cell. If one cell is below 3.5 V at "full", the pack needs replacement.

Closing Up the Chassis

Once all three bench tests pass, install the upper chassis plate.

  1. Route all cables neatly

    Servo cables tucked along the inside of the chassis, away from the standoffs. Battery lead routed to exit through the rear cutout. Nothing should pinch when the top plate seats.

  2. Test-fit the upper plate

    Set it on the standoffs without screws. Confirm nothing protrudes, no cable is trapped, and the plate sits flat.

  3. Secure with eight M2 × 6 mm screws

    Four into the standoffs, four into the corner posts. Snug, not over-torqued; PLA threads strip easily.

  4. Re-test standing and walking through the closed chassis

    The closed robot should behave identically to the open one. If anything has changed (buzzing, drift, a leg behaving differently), a cable or component is likely being pinched or shorted inside. Open the chassis and investigate.

Pre-Flight Checklist

Print this list, tape it inside the lid of the travel case, and confirm every item before departing for the airport.

Bring with you, in working order
  • Assembled and tested quadruped (closed chassis, all twelve servos installed)
  • 2× spare SG90 servos
  • One fully charged 2S Li-Ion battery (two 18650 cells in BH1) plus a second set if you have one
  • 2S charger module compatible with your cells
  • Laptop with Arduino IDE installed and quadruped_base.ino already flashed once
  • USB-C data cable (confirmed working, not power-only)
  • Soldering iron, solder, flux, desolder braid (personal kit)
  • Multimeter
  • Small Phillips and slotted screwdrivers, M2 hex driver if applicable
  • Bag of spare M2 hardware (at least 10 screws of each size used)
  • Your build journal, paper notebook or digital; bring it
  • This manual, printed or on a device that works offline
Warning · Battery air travel

Most airlines treat 2S Li-Ion packs (≈ 7.4 V, < 100 Wh) as carry-on only. Do not pack the battery in checked luggage. Store the cells in a fire-resistant LiPo bag during transit; loose 18650 cells should also have their terminals capped or taped beforehand so they cannot short against other metal objects in the bag. Review the relevant airline's specific rules at least two weeks before departure; Cebu customs has, on occasion, held shipments lacking proper documentation.
