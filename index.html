assembling a quadruped

Welcome, and maayong adlaw. Before you arrive in Cebu for iPBL 2026, you'll build the machine that the rest of the program is built upon: a four-legged, eight-servo robot driven by an Arduino Nano and a custom PCB.

This manual walks you through every screw, every solder joint, every line of firmware. What happens after the robot stands up is a story for when you get here.

Participating Institutions

Three universities. One robot. Eight legs of code.

The International Project-Based Learning programme rotates between member universities each year. For 2026, the University of San Jose – Recoletos in Cebu, Philippines plays host. Students from Taiwan and Japan will travel to join their Filipino counterparts for an intensive collaborative build.

What This Manual Covers

Read this before you fly to Cebu.

This pre-training is deliberately narrow. By the time you land at MCIA, your quadruped should already be standing, blinking, and waiting for instructions. The interesting parts, the parts you didn't expect, begin on Day 01 of the on-ground programme. We've kept them sealed for a reason.

Included in Pre-Training

  • Mechanical assembly of the chassis and eight-servo leg system
  • Recommended 3D-print settings for every structural part
  • PCB schematic walkthrough and hand-soldered assembly
  • Servo wiring, power distribution, and pinout reference
  • Arduino firmware: upload, calibration, and basic gait
  • Bench-testing, troubleshooting, and pre-flight checklist

Reserved for the iPBL Programme

  • Vision and perception layer
  • Higher-level control, behaviour, and autonomy
  • Sensor fusion and environmental interaction
  • The collaborative challenge brief (revealed Day 01)
  • Inter-team protocol and integration tasks

Resist the urge to guess. The surprise is half the project.

Expected Outcomes

What you should arrive with.

By the close of pre-training, every delegate is expected to bring the following to Cebu: physically, intellectually, and in a working state.

  1. A built robot

    Mechanically assembled, with all eight servos installed, calibrated to neutral, and verified.

  2. An assembled PCB

    Either soldered from the kit we shipped, or hand-wired against the published schematic.

  3. Working firmware

    The base sketch uploaded, servos addressable individually, basic gait demonstrated.

  4. A build journal

    A short log of issues encountered and resolved. Bring it. We will read it together.

  5. Spare parts

    At minimum: two spare SG90 servos, extra M2 hardware, and your soldering kit.

  6. A working laptop

    With the Arduino IDE installed, board package configured, and the project sketch flashed at least once.

Begin with the overview.

Eleven sections, two to three weekends of work, one robot that walks. Start at the top and don't skip ahead; every section assumes the one before it.

About this manual

The Quadruped Pre-Training Manual is prepared for delegates of iPBL 2026 by the host team at the University of San Jose – Recoletos. The mechanical platform is based on the open quadruped published by Regis Hsu on PCBWay, adapted and extended for our programme.
