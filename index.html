A field manual for
assembling a quadruped
before day one in Cebu.

Welcome, and maayong adlaw! Before you arrive in Cebu for iPBL 2026, the International Project-Based Learning programme, you'll build the machine that the rest of the program is built upon: a four-legged, twelve-servo robot driven by an Arduino Nano and a custom PCB.

This manual walks you through every screw, every solder joint, every line of firmware. What happens after the robot stands up is a story for when you get here.

Read this before you fly to Cebu.

This pre-training programme is deliberately narrow in scope. Its purpose is to give you a working understanding of what you are about to build, nothing more. Assemble the body, solder the PCB, mount and calibrate the servos, set the rocker arms, and bring the unit to a working crawl: that is the entire brief. By the time you land at MCIA, you should already know how to take a robot from parts to standing, blinking, and awaiting instructions. The more advanced material, the part you have not yet anticipated, begins on Day 01 of the on-ground programme. It remains withheld by design.

Included in Pre-Training

  • Mechanical assembly of the chassis and twelve-servo leg system
  • STL files for every structural part, with a labelled inventory
  • PCB schematic walkthrough and hand-soldered assembly
  • Servo wiring, power distribution, and pinout reference
  • Arduino firmware: upload, calibration, and basic gait
  • A final stand-and-walk check against reference footage

Reserved for the iPBL Programme

  • Vision and perception layer
  • Higher-level control, behaviour, and autonomy
  • Sensor fusion and environmental interaction
  • The collaborative challenge brief (revealed Day 01)
  • Inter-team protocol and integration tasks

What you should arrive knowing.

Nothing you build here is required to travel with you. The quadruped is yours to keep; a fresh kit awaits in Cebu, where you will build again from the beginning. What accompanies you to the programme is what you have learned, and your laptop.

  1. Assembly fluency

    Mechanical assembly of the chassis and twelve-servo leg system, done once already and understood, not just followed.

  2. Soldering and wiring confidence

    A PCB built from the published schematic, kit-soldered or hand-wired, with the pinout committed to memory.

  3. Firmware command

    Comfortable uploading the base sketch, calibrating servos individually, and getting a basic gait running.

  4. Troubleshooting instinct

    A working sense of common failure points: stripped gears, cold solder joints, misaligned rocker arms, so that subsequent builds proceed more efficiently than the first.

  5. A build journal

    A concise personal log of issues encountered and resolved, retained for your own reference; it will not be collected or graded.

  6. A working laptop

    With the Arduino IDE installed, the board package configured, and the project sketch flashed at least once. This is the one item that travels with you to Cebu.

About this manual

The Quadruped Pre-Training Manual is prepared for delegates of iPBL 2026 by the host team at the University of San Jose – Recoletos. The mechanical platform is based on the open quadruped published by Mert Kilic on PCBWay, adapted and extended for our programme.
